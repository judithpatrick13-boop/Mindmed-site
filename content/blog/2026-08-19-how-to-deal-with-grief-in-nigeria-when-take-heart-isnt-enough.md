**Trigger Warning:** This article discusses death and loss.




The One Sentence That Doesn’t Help;

"Take heart."

"God knows best."

"Be strong."




You’ve heard them all. And I know they’re said with love.

But when you’re staring at the ceiling when you’re supposed to be sleeping, those words don’t fill the empty chair at dinner. They don’t pay the school fees. They don’t answer "why".




Grief in Nigeria is loud in the first 7 days. People troop in. Food, prayers, condolences.

Then day 8 comes. Everyone goes back to work. WhatsApp groups go quiet.

And you’re left carrying it alone, trying to act "normal".




If you’re grieving right now; a parent, a partner, a child, a friend, even someone from your street this is for you. You’re not crazy. You’re not weak. You’re grieving. And it’s heavy.




## **What Grief Actually Looks Like**

Hollywood lied to us. Grief isn’t 5 neat stages.




#### **In Your Body**

\- Can’t sleep. Or sleeping 12 hours and still tired

\- No appetite. Or eating everything because food is the only comfort

\- Headaches, chest tightness, random body pain. Doctors say "nothing is wrong"

\- Feeling like you’re in a daze. Driving somewhere and forgetting how you got there




#### **In Your Mind**

\- "Grief brain" is real. Forgetting names, appointments, where you put your keys

\- Anger that comes from nowhere. Angry at God, at the person, at yourself

\- Numbness. Watching your life like a movie with no sound

\- Thinking "what if" on loop. "What if I called that day"




#### **In Your Heart**

\- Waves that hit you in the market, in traffic, hearing a song on the radio

\- Guilt: "I should have called more" "I should have known"

\- Relief mixed with shame, especially after long sickness

\- And sometimes... grief for people you didn’t expect. Like the man on your street. Because his death reminded you of all the other losses you buried.




Grief is not linear. You don’t "get over it". You learn to carry it differently. Some days it’s a backpack. Some days it’s a mountain.




## **Why Grief Feels So Lonely in Nigeria**

#### **1. The 40-Day Rule**

We mourn hard for 7 days, 40 days. There’s aso-ebi, food, prayers.

Then society expects you to "move on". But grief doesn’t check the calendar. Month 3 can be harder than week 1.




#### **2. "Be Strong For Everyone Else"**

You’re the firstborn. You’re the one holding the family together. Who’s holding you?

Crying feels like "burdening" people. So you swallow it.




#### **3. Financial Grief**

When a breadwinner dies, you’re not just grieving a person. You’re grieving rent, school fees, hospital bills, the future you planned.

That pressure makes it impossible to actually grieve. You have to "hustle through it".




#### **4. The Things People Say**

"God needed another angel"

"You’re young, you’ll get married again"

"At least they didn’t suffer"

"Everything happens for a reason"

It’s meant to comfort. It often makes you feel more alone because now you have to pretend you’re okay.




#### **5. No Space to Grieve**

No bereavement leave at most Nigerian jobs. No therapy culture.

So you go to work the next Monday, smile in meetings, and cry in the bathroom.




## **The Truth No One Tells You: Grief Is Weird**

Grief isn’t math. It doesn’t add up.




You can lose 4 people in a year and feel numb. Functioning. "I’m fine."

Then cry for a week because of a neighbor you only said "good morning" to.




That doesn’t mean you loved the others less.

It means one loss can open the door for all the others. Your heart finally has permission to feel.




Sometimes we grieve harder for people who represented hope, or who died suddenly, or who reminded us of ourselves.

Sometimes we grieve what could have been more than what was.




So stop judging how you grieve. There’s no "right" way. No timeline. No quota.




## **How To Cope Without Pretending You’re Fine**

You don’t have to "get over it". But you don’t have to drown either.


**1.Name It**

"I’m grieving." Say it out loud. To yourself. To one safe person. To your journal.

Naming it takes away some of its power. "I’m not lazy. I’m grieving."




**2.Feel It in Small Doses**

Set a timer for 10 minutes. Cry. Look at photos. Write them a letter.

Then do something grounding: cold water on your face, 5-min walk, gospel song, comedy skit.

You don’t have to feel it all at once. That’s how people break.




**3.Talk To Someone Who Can Hold It**

Not "take heart" talk. Real talk.

A therapist, a support group, one friend who can sit with you in silence.

MindMed has therapists who understand grief in a Nigerian context. You don’t have to explain "aso-ebi pressure" to them.




**4.Keep Them Close, Differently**

Cook their food on their birthday. Tell their stories to your kids. Start a small tradition.

Wear their perfume. Play their playlist.

Love doesn’t die. It just changes form.




**5.Basic Care is Not Optional**

Eat something, even if it’s garri. Drink water. Step outside for 5 minutes of sun.

Grief is exhausting. It’s physical labor. Your body needs you to survive this.




**6.Create "Grief Breaks"**

It’s okay to laugh. It’s okay to watch Big Brother.

Taking a break from grief doesn’t mean you’ve forgotten them. It means you’re human.




#### **Grieving Specific Losses in Nigeria**

**Losing a Parent**: The role reversal. Now you’re the one people call. The advice stops.

**Losing a Child**: The silence is the loudest. People don’t know what to say so they say nothing.

**Losing a Partner**: Loneliness and money and "when are you moving on" questions.

**Losing to Suicide**: The guilt, stigma and "why didn’t I see it". Please talk to someone.

**Losing to Sickness**: The trauma of hospitals, and the guilt of relief.




All of it is valid and all of it hurts.




## **When To Get Extra Support**

Grief and depression can look alike. Please reach out if:

\- You haven’t eaten or slept in days and it’s been weeks

\- You’re having thoughts of not wanting to be here

\- You can’t function at work/home after 2-3 months

\- You’re using alcohol/drugs to numb it every day

\- You feel completely cut off from everyone

**You Don’t Have To Be Strong**

You can be a mess. You can be angry at God. You can be okay one day and broken the next.




That’s grief. And that’s human.




One day the waves will come less often. One day you’ll laugh and not feel guilty after. One day you’ll tell their story and smile instead of cry.

But today, just breathe. Just today.

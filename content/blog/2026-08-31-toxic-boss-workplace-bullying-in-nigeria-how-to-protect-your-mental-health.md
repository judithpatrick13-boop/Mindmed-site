You dread Monday from Sunday afternoon.

Your stomach drops when you see his name pop up on your phone.

You’ve started rehearsing conversations in your head before meetings that haven’t even happened yet.

If this sounds familiar, you’re not being “too sensitive.” You might be dealing with a genuinely toxic boss, and in Nigeria, where job scarcity keeps people quiet, that’s a heavier thing to carry than most people admit.

## What A Toxic Boss Actually Looks Like

Not every difficult boss is toxic. Some are just demanding, or having a bad quarter. Toxic is different. It’s a pattern, not a bad day.

**Public humiliation.** Correcting you in front of colleagues in a way that’s designed to shame, not to teach.

**Moving goalposts.** The target changes the moment you’re close to hitting it, so you’re never allowed to actually succeed.

**Taking credit, assigning blame.** Your wins become “the team’s.” Your mistakes become “yours alone,” loudly.

**Unpredictable moods.** You spend energy managing their temperament instead of doing your actual job.

**Isolation.** Cutting you out of information, meetings, or opportunities that would help you grow or leave.

**Guilt-tripping over boundaries.** Making you feel disloyal or ungrateful for wanting normal things: leave days, closing time, a raise in line with inflation.

If several of these are familiar, this isn’t just “a tough job.” It’s a toxic dynamic, and it’s allowed to affect you.

## Why Nigerians Especially Stay Quiet

In many other places, people leave toxic jobs relatively quickly. In Nigeria, the calculation is different, and it’s worth naming honestly.

**Jobs are hard to come by.** With the job market as tight as it is, “just leave” can sound like advice from someone who doesn’t understand your reality. Staying isn’t weakness, it’s often the only realistic option you have right now.

**Family is depending on your salary.** You’re not just protecting yourself, you’re protecting people who count on that alert at month end.

**Speaking up can backlash.** Many workplaces don’t have real HR protection, so reporting a toxic boss can just paint a target on your back instead of fixing anything.

**“At least you have a job.”** This phrase alone has kept so many people silent about genuinely damaging work environments. Having a job doesn’t mean you have to accept everything that comes with it.

None of this means you’re stuck forever. It means the plan has to be realistic, not just “quit tomorrow.”

## How It Affects You, Even If You “Push Through”

A toxic workplace doesn’t stay at work. It follows you home.

You might notice you’re more anxious than you used to be, even outside work hours, our piece on [anxiety vs. stress](https://mindmed.com.ng/blog/Anxiety-vs-stress-how-to-tell-difference/) can help you tell whether what you’re feeling has moved from ordinary work stress into something that needs more attention. You might also notice you’ve stopped enjoying things you used to, snapping at family for no clear reason, or dreading the entire week by Sunday night.

Sleep is often the first thing to go. Then confidence. Then, for many people, a quiet sense that something is wrong with them, rather than with the environment they’re in.

## What You Can Actually Do

**Document everything.** Dates, what was said, who was present. Not to build a court case necessarily, but so you’re not left doubting your own memory later, toxic environments are excellent at making you question whether it was “really that bad.”

**Find your people at work.** One trustworthy colleague who sees what you see can be the difference between feeling crazy and feeling sane. You’re not imagining it if someone else has noticed too.

**Protect your evenings.** If you can’t yet leave the job, protect what’s left. Turn off work notifications after a set time. Guard at least one part of your day that’s fully yours.

**Separate their behavior from your worth.** A toxic boss’s opinion of you is not an accurate report card. It’s often more about their own pressure, insecurity, or management failures than about your actual performance.

Start quietly building an exit, even a slow one. Update your CV. Reconnect with your network. You don’t need a plan to leave tomorrow, you need to know you’re not trapped forever.

## When To See A Therapist

Consider booking a session if:

•	You dread going to work most days, not just occasionally

•	You’ve noticed physical symptoms, headaches, stomach issues, trouble sleeping, tied to work

•	Your confidence has taken a real hit since this started

•	You’re not sure anymore whether you’re overreacting or genuinely being mistreated

If any of that sounds familiar, it might be worth taking the signs you need therapy seriously rather than waiting for things to get worse before reaching out.

## You’re Not Weak For Struggling With This

Surviving a toxic workplace takes real strength, even when it doesn’t feel like it from the inside.

Wanting it to stop doesn’t mean you can’t handle pressure. It means you can tell the difference between pressure and mistreatment, and that’s clarity, not weakness.

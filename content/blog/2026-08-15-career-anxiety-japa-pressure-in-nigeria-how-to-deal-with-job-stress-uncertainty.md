Sunday night. Your chest is tight. Three people from your team have JAPAed this year. LinkedIn is full of “New Role: Canada” posts. And you’re here wondering: am I stupid for staying?

If this is you, you’re not alone.

In 2026, career anxiety in Nigeria isn’t just about “I hate my job.” It’s about: “Should I stay and build here, or leave and start over?” That’s a heavy question to carry alone. Let’s talk about it.

“Everyone Is Leaving”: The New Career Anxiety in Nigeria

## There are two types of career anxiety right now.

**Type 1: The Job Stress.** Toxic boss. Unrealistic KPIs. No raise despite inflation. Burnout. You go to work and feel drained. This one isn’t new, Nigerian workplaces have always asked a lot, but inflation and the cost of living have made a bad job feel a lot more trapping than it used to.

**Type 2: The JAPA Pressure.** Your best developer left. Your mentor left. Your WhatsApp group is “Canada Things.” Now work is harder because you’re doing two people’s jobs. And you feel guilt, FOMO, and “what if I’m missing my chance?” This pressure doesn’t only come from watching others leave, it comes from the quiet question of whether staying means you’re falling behind, even when you know that’s not really true.

Most Nigerians are dealing with both at once. That’s why it feels so overwhelming.

## What Career Anxiety + JAPA Pressure Actually Feels Like

It’s not just stress. It looks like:

**1.	Sunday Scaries** — dread starting from 6pm Sunday

**2.	Comparison Spiral** — two hours scrolling LinkedIn, feeling like a failure

**3.	Decision Paralysis** — “Stay, JAPA, switch career, upskill” and your brain freezes

**4.	Survivor’s Guilt** — “Why did they get a visa and not me?”

**5.	Resentment** — at the company, at the country, at yourself

**6.	Physical symptoms** — headaches, no sleep, stomach issues before work

If three or more of these feel familiar, your nervous system is telling you something. If you’re not sure whether what you’re feeling is everyday stress or something that’s tipped into anxiety, our piece on [anxiety vs. stress](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/Anxiety-vs-stress-how-to-tell-difference/) can help you tell the difference.

## “Should I JAPA Or Stay?” 3 Questions To Ask Yourself

Everyone will give you advice. Your aunty, your boss, Twitter. But this is your life. Ask yourself:

**Am I running FROM something or running TO something?** Running from a toxic job is valid. Running to “Canada will fix everything” is a fantasy. No country fixes anxiety.

**What would “success” look like here in two years?** If you stayed, what’s one thing you could build? Sometimes we don’t stay because we never tried to grow here.

**Can I make this decision from peace, not panic?** Panic decisions lead to regret. If you’re deciding out of fear, pause. Talk to someone first.

There’s no “right” answer. Some people should JAPA. Some people should stay and build. Both are brave.

## 5 Ways To Cope With Career Anxiety Whether You Stay Or Go

**Separate your worth from your location.** You are not “less than” because you’re in Nigeria. And you’re not “more” because you’re abroad. Write down three skills you have that work in any country.

**Do a “comparison detox.”** Mute LinkedIn for 30 days. Unfollow accounts that make you feel small. The people posting wins are not posting visa rejections and loneliness.

**Make one small move.** Anxiety loves “I don’t know.” Action kills it. This week: update your CV, take one course, talk to one person in your target role, or research one visa option. Momentum beats perfection.

**Talk about it.** Don’t carry “should I JAPA” alone. It’s too heavy. Talk to a mentor, friend, or therapist. Saying it out loud removes the shame.

**Protect your mental health at work now.** Set boundaries. Take lunch breaks. Ask for help with workload. You can’t make a big life decision from burnout.

## When To See A Therapist For Career Help

Therapy isn’t just for “mental illness.” It’s for big decisions.

Book a session if:

1.	You’ve been anxious about work or JAPA for two or more months

2.	You can’t sleep or you’re crying before work

3.	You and your partner or family are fighting about “stay or go”

4.	You feel stuck and need clarity, not just advice

The 25-year-old CEO on Instagram is the exception, not the rule. Some people JAPA at 24. Some at 40. Some build amazing lives here.

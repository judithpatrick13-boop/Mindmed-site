"I’m so tired of being strong."




"I cancel plans. My friends stopped inviting me. My boss thinks I’m lazy. And the worst part? Sometimes I think they’re right."




If you live with cancer, lupus, sickle cell, diabetes, or any chronic illness in Nigeria, you know that sentence.




Because the pain isn’t just in your body. It’s in your mind too.




## **The Loop No One Talks About: Body → Mind → Body**




Chronic illness doesn’t just give you symptoms. It changes your whole life.




Here’s how the loop works:

1. **Pain & Fatigue:** Your body is in fight mode 24/7. Poor sleep, brain fog, flare-ups.

2. **Anxiety:** "What if I have a crisis at work?" "What if this is the chemo that stops working?" "What if my sugar spikes tonight?"

3. **Depression & Grief:** You grieve the life you thought you’d have. The job, the wedding, the travel, the "normal" body.

4. **Shame:** "I’m a burden." "I’m not doing enough."

5. **Worse Physical Health:** Stress and depression increase inflammation, pain, and make it harder to manage your condition.




Research shows people with chronic illnesses are 2-3x more likely to experience anxiety and depression. That’s not "being dramatic." That’s biology + psychology.




## **"But You Don’t Look Sick": The Cost of Invisible Illness in Nigeria**




Now add Nigeria to it.




**Cancer:** You’re fighting for chemo drugs, explaining to family why you lost your hair, and hearing "just have faith" when you’re terrified.




**Sickle Cell:** Pain crises that land you in A&E. People calling you "carrier" like it’s an insult. The fear every time the weather changes.




**Lupus:** Flares that keep you in bed for weeks. "You don’t look sick." Friends who disappear because you cancel too much.




**Diabetes:** Checking sugar in public. The cost of insulin. Family saying "stop eating rice" like that fixes everything. Fear of complications.




And across all of them: the financial stress. The hospital bills. The "why me" questions in church. The pressure to "pray it away" and "be strong for others."




So you learn to hide it. You show up to owambe and smile. You go to work and push through pain.

That’s called masking. And it’s exhausting.




## **The Mental Health Impact We Don’t Name**




Let’s name it:




#### **1. Grief**

You’re grieving the healthy version of you. The 5-year plan. The body that didn’t betray you. Grief is real, even if you’re still alive.




#### **2. Anxiety**

Health anxiety is valid when your health is actually unpredictable. It’s not in your head. It’s your nervous system trying to protect you.




#### **3. Depression**

Chronic pain, inflammation, and isolation directly affect serotonin and dopamine. Feeling hopeless doesn’t mean you’re weak. It means you’re human.




#### **4. Medical Trauma**

Being dismissed by doctors. "It’s just stress." Long waits. Bad news delivered badly. That creates real trauma.




#### **5. Isolation**

People don’t know what to say. So they say nothing. You pull away to avoid explaining. Now you’re alone with it.




## **3 Truths If You’re Living With Chronic Illness Right Now**




#### **1. This Is Not Your Fault**

You didn’t cause your cancer. You didn’t "pray less" and get lupus. Sickle cell is genetics. Diabetes is complex.

Shame makes illness heavier. Put it down.




#### **2. Mental Health Care IS Medical Care**

Therapy, support groups, and medication don’t cure your physical illness. But they improve how you cope with it.

Studies show that treating depression in people with diabetes or cancer leads to better blood sugar control and better treatment adherence.

Your mind and body are connected. Treating both is smart.




#### **3. You Can Redefine a "Good Life"**

The life you planned might not happen. That hurts. But a new life can still be full.

Full of small joys. Full of people who get it. Full of meaning that isn’t tied to productivity.

You are not "less than" because your capacity is different.




## **What Actually Helps: Tools That Work in Nigeria**




#### **1. Find the Right Support**

You need 2 teams: Medical + Mental.

Ask your doctor for a referral to the hospital’s psych department. Many teaching hospitals in Lagos, Abuja, Ibadan now have them.

Therapy helps with: coping skills, grief, anxiety, talking to family, and trauma.




#### **2. Practice Pacing, Not Pushing**

Spoon Theory is real. You have limited energy. Spend it on what matters. Rest is not laziness. It's a treatment.




#### **3. Build Your Scripts**

**For family:** "I appreciate you. I’m managing a chronic illness and I need support, not solutions right now."

**For work:** "I have a medical condition that requires flexibility. Here’s how I can still deliver."

Boundaries protect your peace.




#### **4. Join Community**

You are not the only one. Sickle Cell support groups, Diabetes associations, Cancer support groups exist in Nigeria. Online communities too. Shame dies in community.

Read our guide on [how to support someone you love through grief or depression](https://mindmed.com.ng/blog/how-to-support-someone-grief-depression-nigeria/).




#### **5. Talk About It**

Name it: "I’m anxious today because of my next scan."

Journal it. Voice note a friend. Tell your therapist. What’s named loses power.




## **FAQ**




#### **Q: Is it normal to feel depressed because of my illness?**

A: Yes. 100%. Depression is a common medical complication of chronic illness. It’s not a weakness. Please talk to your doctor and a therapist.




#### **Q: My family says I should just pray and stop worrying. What do I do?**

A: Faith and medicine can work together. You can pray and go to therapy. You can pray and take your medication. Both are care. 

Read our guide to learn [how to set healthy boundaries with family in Nigeria](https://mindmed.com.ng/blog/how-to-set-boundaries-with-family-nigeria/).




#### **Q: Does therapy help if my illness can’t be cured?**

A: Yes. Therapy helps you live well with your illness. It helps with pain management, anxiety, relationships, and finding meaning.

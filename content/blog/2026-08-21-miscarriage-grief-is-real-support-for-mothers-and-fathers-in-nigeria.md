If you’re reading this, I’m so sorry.




Maybe someone told you "At least it was early" or "God knows best" or "You can try again."

Maybe you’re smiling at work but crying in the bathroom.

Maybe your husband is quiet and you don’t know how to reach him.




Let me say this first: **What you lost was real. And your grief is real.**




Whether it was 6 weeks or 16 weeks. Whether it was your first or third. Losing a pregnancy is losing a future you already started loving.




And to the fathers reading this: You lost it too. You’re allowed to feel it.




## **"At Least It Was Early" And Other Things People Get Wrong**



This is the one we hear most. And it’s not true.

Grief isn’t measured in weeks. The moment you saw those two lines, or heard the heartbeat, or picked baby names, you started bonding.




Losing early doesn’t mean it hurts less. Sometimes it hurts more because people expect you to "just move on."




Other harmful things people say:

1. **"Everything happens for a reason"** - You don’t need a reason. You need space to hurt.

2. "**You can have another one"** - That baby was not replaceable.

3. **"Be strong"** - Especially to men. Strength includes crying.




You don’t have to agree with any of that. You’re allowed to be angry, numb, guilty, or empty.

## **What Miscarriage Grief Actually Looks Like**




It’s not just sadness. It can be:

1. **Shock and numbness** - The first 1-2 weeks

2. **Guilt** - "Did I do something wrong? Was it the food, the stress?"

3. **Anxiety** - Fear about trying again

4. **Anger** - At your body, at God, at people who don’t get it

5. **Jealousy** - Seeing pregnancy announcements




For women: There’s also the physical pain + hormone crash on top of emotional pain.

For men: There’s often the pain of watching your partner hurt and feeling helpless.




Both are valid. Both are heavy.




## **For Fathers: Your Grief Matters Too**




Nigerian men are told to "hold the family together." But you lost something too.




You might feel:

1. **Helpless** - You couldn’t protect her or the baby

2. **Invisible** - Everyone is asking "how is she?" and no one asks you

3. **Pressure to be strong** - So you push it down




Pushing it down doesn’t make it go away. It shows up as irritability, drinking, working too much, or shutting down.




Talk to someone. A friend, pastor, therapist. Cry if you need to. Your partner needs you, but she also needs the real you — not the "strong" version.




You’re grieving. Say it.




## **How To Support Each Other As A Couple**




Grief can pull couples apart if you grieve differently. Here’s how to stay close:




#### **1. Name it together**

"That was our baby. We’re both hurting." Saying it out loud matters.




#### **2. Ask, don’t assume**

She might need to talk. You might need silence. Ask: "What do you need from me today?"




#### **3. Take pressure off**

No sex, no "try again" talk, no big decisions for a while. Just heal.




#### **4. Create a small ritual**

Light a candle. Write a letter. Plant something. It helps to acknowledge the loss.




#### **5. Get help if you’re stuck**

If after 4-6 weeks you can’t eat, sleep, or function, or if you’re fighting constantly, therapy helps.




## **What To Say And What Not To Say**




#### **Say this:**

\- "I’m so sorry. I’m here."

\- "This is not your fault."

\- "Do you want to talk or just sit together?"

\- "I’m grieving too."




#### **Don’t say this:**

\- "At least it was early"

\- "You can try again"

\- "God has a plan"

\- "Be strong"




Sometimes the best thing is: \`I don’t know what to say, but I’m not going anywhere.\`




## **When To Get Professional Support**




Reach out to a therapist or counselor if:

1. The sadness isn’t lifting after 6-8 weeks

2. You’re having panic attacks or can’t function

3. You’re blaming each other or withdrawing completely

4. You had previous trauma or depression

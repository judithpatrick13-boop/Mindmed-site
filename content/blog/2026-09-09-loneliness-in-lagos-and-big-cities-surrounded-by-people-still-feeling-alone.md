You’re in Lagos.

Traffic on 3rd Mainland. 100 people in your office, 500 people in your church. 2,000 followers online.




So why do you feel so alone?




If you’ve ever sat in your room in Yaba, Lekki, or Ikeja and thought “I’m surrounded by people but nobody really knows me", this is for you.




Loneliness in a big city is a special kind of pain. Because everyone assumes you can’t be lonely here.




## **Why Big Cities Make Us Feel More Lonely**




It’s not just you. City life is wired to disconnect us.




#### **1. "Surface-level everything"**

We have 100 acquaintances but 0 people we can call at 2am.

Small talk in the bus. "How far" in the office. But no real "how are you, really?"




#### **2. Hustle culture**

Everyone is chasing money, japa, promotion.

"Sorry I’m busy" becomes the default. Friendships start feeling like another task on your to-do list.




#### **3. Transience**

People relocate. Colleagues leave. Flatmates change every 6 months.

It’s hard to build roots when everyone is trying to leave.




#### **4. Comparison on social media**

You see brunch in Lekki, baecation in Dubai, "my village people" memes.

Your brain: "Everyone has community except me."




#### **5. Living alone & commuting alone**

2 hours in traffic. Door to room to bed. Repeat.

You can go days without a real conversation.




That’s not a weakness. That’s city design.




#### **What City Loneliness Actually Feels Like**




It’s not always crying in a corner. Sometimes it’s:




**The Sunday evening dread:** The city is quiet and your chest feels heavy

**The group chat silence:** You see people making plans and you weren’t invited

**The "I’m fine" mask:** At work you’re productive, but at night it hits

**The scrolling:** 3 hours on TikTok because the silence is too loud

**The numbness:** "I should feel something, but I don’t"




Sound familiar? You’re not broken, you’re human.




## **5 Things That Actually Help With City Loneliness**




Not "just go out more". Real things.




#### **1. Name it without shame**

Say: "I feel lonely."

Loneliness is a signal, like hunger. It means you need connection not  that you’re unlovable.




#### **2. Trade "big friends" for "small moments"**

You don’t need 5 besties overnight.

Start with: The buka aunty you greet by name. The colleague you ask "you good?" The neighbor you share light with.

Micro-connections add up.




#### **3. Join something with repeated contact**

One-off events don’t work. You need to see the same faces.

Book club, gym class, church/mosque group, volunteer team, MindMed group therapy.

Repetition builds familiarity. Familiarity builds safety.




#### **4. Talk to someone who’s paid to listen**

Friends are great, but they’re also tired.

A therapist is a space where it’s ALL about you. No performance. No "you’re too much".

Just 50 minutes where you’re fully seen.




#### **5. Limit the scroll, increase the real**

For every 30 mins on social media, try 10 mins of real contact. Call your mum. Voice note a friend. Sit outside.

Your brain needs real faces, not just screens.




## **"But I Have People Around Me"**




This is the city paradox.




You can be in a relationship and lonely.

You can live with roommates and be lonely.

You can be "the funny one" in the group and lonely.




Because loneliness is not about being alone. It’s about feeling unseen.

If no one knows the real you; your fears, your wins, your boring Tuesday, you’ll feel disconnected.

It’s only November and the messages have started.

“Are you travelling this year?” “Detty December plans?” “Who is hosting?” Your aunty has already asked if you’re coming home with “good news.”

If your chest tightened reading that, you’re not being dramatic. December is the hardest month of the year for a lot of Nigerians, and people talk about it far less than they should.

## Why December Hits So Hard

**Money.** Travel, gifts, “village rice,” contributions, new clothes for the children, aso-ebi for three owambes. Meanwhile January school fees and rent are already waiting. You spend in December and panic in January.

**Family questions.** “When are you getting married?” “Any baby?” “Where are you working now?” “Why aren’t you abroad yet?” Questions asked with love can still hurt. If this is you, read our piece on [being 30+ and single in Nigeria](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/30-and-single-nigeria-anxiety/).

**Comparison.** A cousin lands from Canada with photos and gifts. Instagram is full of December wins. You start measuring your year against everyone else’s highlights. We wrote about the [anxiety that comes with JAPA](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/japa-mental-health-nigeria/) from both sides.

**Loneliness.** The city empties as people travel. Friends have japa-ed. You’re in Lagos with the generator and your phone. More on that in [loneliness in big cities](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/loneliness-lagos-big-cities/).

**Grief.** The empty chair at the table. The first December after a loss can feel unbearable. See [how to deal with grief in Nigeria](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/how-to-deal-with-grief-nigeria/).

**Work.** Year-end targets, appraisals, and covering for colleagues who are on leave. No break for you.

**The year-end report card.** “What did I achieve this year?” And the honest answer feels like “nothing.”

## Signs It’s More Than Stress

- Dreading the month weeks before it starts
- Short temper, snapping at people you love
- Trouble sleeping, or sleeping all day
- Drinking more than usual, or spending to feel better
- Crying out of nowhere
- Avoiding calls, invitations, or going home
- Headaches, tight chest, stomach problems

## How To Get Through It

**1. Set your December number now.** Decide what you can spend on travel, gifts and contributions before anyone asks. Write it down. When someone asks for more, say: “This is what I can do this year.” Then stop talking. Giving beyond your means isn’t generosity, it’s debt with a smile. See [money anxiety in Nigeria](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/money-anxiety-nigeria/).

**2. Prepare your answers.** You don’t owe anyone a full update on your life. Have two or three lines ready:
- “I’ll let you know when there’s news.”
- “Thank you for caring. How is your business?”
- “I’d rather not talk about that today.”

Smile, redirect, and go and eat.

**3. Plan your exit.** Decide how long you’ll stay. If you can, sleep somewhere of your own. Take small breaks: help in the kitchen, take a walk, sit in the car for ten minutes. More on this in [how to set boundaries with family](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/how-to-set-boundaries-with-family-nigeria/).

**4. Keep one normal thing.** A regular sleep time. One proper meal a day. A short walk. When everything else is chaos, routine keeps you steady.

**5. Make room for the person you’re missing.** Say their name. Cook their food, play their music, light a candle, visit the grave if you can. It’s easier to plan a moment for them than to be ambushed by grief at the dinner table.

**6. If you’re staying alone, plan it.** Pick two things to look forward to: a meal with a friend, a long video call, a church or community event, a proper home-cooked dinner. Message someone before the day, not after.

**7. Mute the comparison.** Mute accounts for the month. Photos are highlights, not the full story.

**8. Do a kinder year-end review.** Instead of listing what you failed to do, write down three things you got through. This has been a hard year in Nigeria. Getting through it counts.

**9. Go easy on the drink.** Alcohol is everywhere in December and it can make anxiety and low mood worse. If you’re already leaning on it, read [alcohol, tramadol and codeine in Nigeria](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/alcohol-tramadol-addiction-nigeria/).

## If Going Home Isn’t Safe Or Healthy

You’re allowed to skip it. If a relative belittles you, hurts you, or going home leaves you ill for weeks, you can send a gift, join a video call, or visit for one day. Duty to family doesn’t mean accepting harm. If you are ever in danger, go to a safe place first.

Nobody shouts at you. Nobody insults you in front of the whole office.

But you drive home drained in a way you can’t explain. You replay conversations in your head. Was that a joke? Did she mean it that way? Maybe you’re just too sensitive.

That doubt is part of how subtle bullying works.

If you’ve read our piece on the [toxic boss and workplace bullying](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/toxic-boss-workplace-bullying-nigeria/) and thought “mine isn’t that loud,” this one is for you.

## Why It’s So Hard To Spot

Subtle bullying is deniable. Each incident is small. Each one comes with an excuse: “I was joking.” “I forgot.” “That’s just how I am.”

Spread over months, it adds up to a pattern. In Nigeria, a few things help it hide:

- Respect for seniority means we explain away what “the oga” does.
- “At least you have a job” makes us stay quiet.
- “Banter” is common, so cruelty gets passed off as humour.

## 10 Signs You Might Be Missing

**1. The “joke” always lands on you.** Your accent, tribe, hair, weight, relationship status, age. When you push back: “Relax, we’re just joking. You’re too sensitive.”

**2. Backhanded compliments.** “You’re very sharp for someone who didn’t study abroad.” “I didn’t expect you to do this well.”

**3. You’re left out “by mistake.”** Not added to the WhatsApp group. The meeting time changes and no one tells you. “Oh, we forgot to add you.” Again.

**4. Your ideas disappear, then come back.** You speak in a meeting and get ignored. Ten minutes later a colleague says the same thing and gets praised.

**5. Silent treatment.** Greetings not returned. One-word replies. Conversations stop when you walk in.

**6. Double standards.** Everyone comes late, but your lateness is written down. Small mistakes from you become big issues. Rules are enforced only on you.

**7. You’re set up to fail.** Impossible deadlines. Information or access withheld, then you’re blamed for missing it. Real work taken away and replaced with errands, or the opposite, a pile that no one else would carry.

**8. Gaslighting.** “I never said that.” “You’re remembering it wrong.” “You’re overreacting.” You start keeping screenshots because you no longer trust your own memory.

**9. Quiet gossip.** Rumours about your private life. People calling you “difficult” without saying why. Colleagues suddenly cold with no explanation.

**10. Your “tone” becomes the problem.** Not what you said, but how. “You don’t respect elders.” “Why are you so aggressive?” “Smile more.” Women hear this a lot. It turns disagreement into disrespect.

## Bullying Or Just A Bad Day?

Everyone has bad days at work. One rude comment isn’t bullying. Ask yourself:

- **Is it a pattern?** Repeated over weeks or months.
- **Is it aimed at you?** Are others treated differently?
- **Is there always an excuse?** Every incident can be explained away.
- **What does it do to you?** Do you feel smaller, anxious, or dread going in?

If you answered yes to most of these, trust what you’re noticing.

## What It Does To You

Subtle bullying wears you down slowly. You may start doubting your memory and your skills. You stay alert for the next comment. You can’t sleep. You get headaches or stomach problems. You lose confidence in things you were good at last year. Over time, this can lead to [burnout and insomnia](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/burnout-insomnia-nigeria/).

## What You Can Do

**Write it down.** In a private note: date, what happened, exact words, who was there. This also helps you check whether a pattern really exists. Our toxic boss guide has more on keeping records.

**Check your read with one safe person.** A friend or family member who isn’t at your workplace can tell you if it sounds normal or not.

**Put things in writing.** After a verbal instruction, send a short message: “Just to confirm what we agreed today...” It creates a record and makes “I never said that” harder.

**Name it once, calmly.** “I’d like us to stop making jokes about my accent.” Short. No long explanation. Whether this is safe depends on your workplace and who you’re dealing with, and only you can judge that.

**Know your options.** Check your staff handbook for a grievance process. Be honest with yourself: many workplaces here have weak HR protection, so think about who you’d report to and what it could cost you before you do.

**Protect your energy outside work.** Switch off work notifications. Spend time with people who don’t make you question yourself.

**Quietly plan an exit if nothing changes.** Update your CV. Talk to people in your field. Leaving a workplace that’s harming you is not failure.

## When To See A Therapist

Consider a session if:

- You dread work from Sunday afternoon
- You doubt yourself more than you used to
- You can’t sleep or you cry before work
- You’re turning to alcohol or pills to cope
- You can’t decide whether to stay or leave

Therapy won’t replace a workplace grievance process, but it can help you test whether your read is accurate, practise what to say, and decide what to do next.

"Would I look better if I was lighter?"

"I don’t like my skin in photos."

"I’ve tried 5 creams this month. Nothing is working."




If you’ve thought this, you’re not vain. You’re not "shallow."

You’re responding to pressure that’s been here for years.




In Nigeria, conversations about skin bleaching usually turn into judgment: "It’s dangerous." "Love yourself."

But no one talks about what’s underneath: body dysmorphia, anxiety, and the belief that your worth is tied to your shade.




Let’s talk about it.




## **What’s Really Going On: Bleaching and Body Image**




**Skin bleaching i**s using creams, soaps, pills, or injections to lighten skin tone.

Common ingredients: hydroquinone, mercury, steroids.




**Body Dysmorphic Disorder/BDD**  is when you become obsessed with a "flaw" in your appearance that others barely notice. You check mirrors, avoid photos, compare constantly, and feel deep shame about how you look.




They’re connected.




Because bleaching often starts with a thought: "If I was lighter, I’d be prettier. I’d get the job. He’d love me more. People would respect me."

That’s not about skincare. That’s about self-worth.




## **Why It’s So Common in Nigeria**




It’s not just "personal choice". The pressure is everywhere:




1**. Colorism:** "Yellow pawpaw". "Fair is beautiful". Darker shades are called "black beauty" but are still teased as "too dark".

**2. Media:** Most ads, TV presenters, and influencers with the biggest deals are lighter skinned.

**3. Dating & Marriage:** "I want a light-skinned wife." "Don’t bring a dark girl home."

**4. Jobs:** Some industries reward "brighter" complexions.

**5. Social Media:** Filters that lighten you automatically. Comments like "you’d be fine if you were lighter".




So you grow up learning: lighter = better, cleaner, more valuable.

That message gets into your head. And it hurts.




## **The Mental Health Side No One Names**




Here’s what bleaching and BDD can look like:




**1. Obsession:** Checking your skin 20x a day. Only using certain filters. Avoiding sunlight.

**2. Anxiety:** "What if I get darker?" "What if people notice I stopped?"

**3. Depression & Shame:** Feeling like your natural skin isn’t enough. Hiding from people.

**4. Physical Harm:** Thinning skin, rashes, stretch marks, kidney damage from mercury/steroids. Then more shame.

**5. The Cycle:** Cream stops working → buy stronger cream → more damage → more insecurity




It’s a loop. And it’s exhausting.




## **3 Truths If You’re Struggling With This Right Now**




#### **1. You Are Not Bad For Wanting To Be Lighter**

You learned that. From TV, family, comments, the world. Wanting to fit in is human.

Shame makes it worse. Compassion helps you heal.




#### **2. Bleaching Is Often A Coping Mechanism**

For anxiety. For rejection. For "I don’t feel good enough."

It makes sense that you tried it. But there are safer ways to feel confident.




#### **3. Your Worth Is Not Measured In Shades**

You are not "too dark". You are not "not yellow enough".

You are whole at the skin you’re in. And you deserve to feel that.




## **What Actually Helps: Moving Forward**




#### **1. Name It Without Shame**

Say: "I’m struggling with how I feel about my skin." To a therapist, friend, or in a journal.

What’s hidden grows. What’s named can be worked on.




#### **2. Get Medical Help To Stop Safely**

Don’t just quit strong steroid creams cold turkey. See a dermatologist. They can help you repair your skin barrier safely.

Nigeria has good derms in teaching hospitals.




#### **3. Therapy Works For This**

This is exactly what therapy is for.

**\- CBT:** Challenges thoughts like "I’m ugly because I’m dark"

**\- Self-compassion work:** Learning to talk to yourself kindly

**\- BDD treatment:** Reducing mirror checking, photo avoidance

A therapist won’t tell you "just love yourself". They’ll help you get there.




#### **4. Curate What You See**

Unfollow accounts that make you feel bad about your skin. Follow creators with different shades. Limit filters that lighten you.




#### **5. Build Identity Outside Looks**

What are you good at? What do you care about? Pour into that. Confidence comes from competence, not just complexion.




## **How To Support Someone Who Bleaches**




Don’t say: "You’re ruining your skin" "You have self-hate"

Do say: "I’m worried about you. Can we talk about what’s making you feel like you need to lighten?"

Offer: "Want me to help you find a dermatologist?" "Want to see a therapist together?"




You can’t shame someone into self-love.

#### **When To Get Professional Help**




Reach out if:

\- You can’t stop thinking about your skin/shade

\- You avoid social events, photos, or mirrors

\- You’re using dangerous products and can’t stop

\- You feel depressed, anxious, or worthless because of how you look




This is not vanity. This is mental health.

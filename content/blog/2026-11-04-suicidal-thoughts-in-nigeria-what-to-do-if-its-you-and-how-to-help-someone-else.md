*Trigger warning: this article discusses suicide.*

> **If you are thinking about ending your life right now, or you’re worried someone will act on it:** call [112](tel:112) (or [767](tel:767) in Lagos), or go to the nearest hospital emergency unit. If you can, don’t be alone. Tell someone near you.
>
> You can also call these free lines: **She Writes Woman** [0800 800 2000](tel:08008002000), **SURPIN** [0800 078 7746](tel:08000787746), **Lagos Lifeline** [0700 000 6463](tel:07000006463).

If you’re reading this at 2am, we’re glad you’re here.

You don’t have to be sure you’re “serious enough” to reach out. You don’t need a perfect reason. If the thought has crossed your mind, it deserves attention.

## What Suicidal Thoughts Can Feel Like

They don’t always look like “I want to die.” Sometimes it’s:

- “I just want the pain to stop.”
- “Everyone would be better off without me.”
- “What if I just disappeared?”
- A heavy numbness where nothing matters
- Thoughts that are quiet and passing, or ones that feel loud and constant

Any of these is worth talking about. Having these thoughts doesn’t mean you’re weak, selfish, or “attention seeking.” They often come when pain feels bigger than your ability to cope. Depression, grief, money stress, abuse, addiction, burnout, shame, and loneliness can all feed them.

In Nigeria there’s an extra layer. People say it’s a spiritual attack, or that you lack faith, or that you should “be strong.” Many are afraid of being judged, or of the law.

## About The Law

Attempting suicide is still a crime on paper in Nigeria: Section 327 of the Criminal Code in southern states and Section 231 of the Penal Code in the north, with up to a year in prison. In August 2026 the Federal Executive Council approved a change to decriminalise it, and a bill is before the Senate, but as of November 2026 it is not law yet.

The Mental Health Act already treats attempted suicide as a medical emergency. Help lines, doctors and therapists are there to support you. Please don’t let fear of the law stop you from asking for help, or from getting help for someone else.

## If It’s You

**1. Tell someone today.** One person. A friend, sibling, a trusted colleague, a pastor or imam you trust, or a therapist. You don’t need the right words. “I’m not okay. I’ve been having thoughts about not wanting to be alive” is enough.

**2. Call a line.** The numbers are at the top. You don’t have to explain everything. You can say “I don’t know what to say.”

**3. Put distance between you and anything you could use to hurt yourself.** Ask someone to keep it for now, or move it out of the house.

**4. Don’t be alone tonight.** Go to someone’s place, or ask someone to come over.

**5. Get through the next hour.** Strong urges often rise and fall. Do one small thing: drink water, eat something, text someone, sit near people.

**6. Stay away from alcohol and drugs tonight.** They lower your guard and can make things worse.

**7. Get proper help.** Depression and anxiety are treatable. A doctor or therapist can help you work out what’s behind this and how to feel different. ## If It’s Someone You Love

**Warning signs:**
- Talking about death, not wanting to be here, or being a burden
- Giving away belongings, or saying goodbye in odd ways
- Pulling away from people
- Big mood changes, including sudden calm after a long period of distress
- Drinking or using more
- Hopelessness about the future

**Ask directly.** “Are you thinking about suicide?” Asking doesn’t put the idea in their head. It often brings relief, because someone finally said it out loud.

**Listen.** Don’t argue or fix. Let them talk. “I’m glad you told me. I’m here.”

**Avoid these lines:**
- “Think of your children.” (It can deepen the feeling of being a burden.)
- “It’s a spiritual attack, just pray.”
- “Others have it worse.”
- “You’re strong, you’ll be fine.”
- “That’s selfish.”

Prayer and faith can be a real support. They go alongside medical care, not in place of it.

**Stay.** If you think they might act soon, don’t leave them alone. Help them move anything dangerous out of reach.

**Get help.** Call one of the numbers above together, or take them to a hospital emergency unit. If the danger is immediate, call 112.

**Follow up.** Check in the next day and the week after. The days after a crisis matter.

## If You’ve Lost Someone To Suicide

It’s not your fault, even if your mind says you should have known. Grief after suicide often comes with guilt, anger, and questions that have no answers. You may also feel judged by others.

You saw the message. Or someone told you. Or you’ve known for months and finally said it out loud.

Within an hour, your aunty is on the phone. “Don’t do anything rash.” “He’s a man.” “Think of the children.” Your family WhatsApp group knows before you’ve eaten.

If you’re the one who was cheated on, this is for you. If you’re the one who cheated, or the one deciding whether to stay or go, there’s something here for you too.

## What It Does To You

Finding out can feel like being hit. Common reactions:

- Shock, nausea, no appetite
- Can’t sleep, or can’t stop replaying it
- Checking their phone, going through old messages
- Rage one minute, tears the next
- Shame: “What’s wrong with me?”
- Sometimes, strange relief that you finally know

These are normal reactions to a major betrayal. Your brain and body are in alarm mode. Cheating is a choice the other person made. Whatever else was wrong in the marriage, it didn’t force that choice.

## The First Few Weeks: What To Do And Avoid

**Don’t make big decisions at the peak of the pain.** Staying, leaving, moving out, telling everyone. Give yourself time.

**Don’t confront in front of the children or the family.** Or post it on WhatsApp status or social media. You can’t take it back.

**Tell one person.** One friend or sibling who will hold it quietly, not the whole family.

**Look after your body.** Eat something. Drink water. Sleep as much as you can.

**Get tested if there was unprotected sex outside the relationship.** This is about your health, and it’s a reasonable thing to ask for.

**If you’re afraid of how your partner will react,** treat it as a safety issue. Read our guide on [abusive relationships](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/domestic-violence-abusive-relationship-nigeria/) and get help before confronting anyone.

## When Family, Church And Culture Say “Endure”

You’ll hear many things:

- “Men will be men.”
- “A woman builds her home.”
- “Forgive seventy times seven.”
- “What will people say?”
- “The children need two parents.”
- For men: “You can’t control your own wife?”

Some of this comes from love, and some from fear of scandal. But you’re the one living in the marriage.

Forgiving is not the same as staying. And staying is not the same as being okay. You can forgive and still leave. You can stay and still need time.

## Stay Or Go: Questions To Ask Yourself

There’s no single right answer.

1. **Is the affair over, and is the person honest?** Rebuilding trust needs the cheating to stop and the lies to stop.
2. **Do they take responsibility?** Or do they blame you, the stress, or “the devil”?
3. **Are you safe?** Physically, and financially.
4. **Can you picture trusting them again,** even slowly?
5. **What do you want your life to look like in five years?**
6. **What does this do for the children?** Not “stay for the children,” but what actually protects them: a calm home, whether together or apart.

Some marriages recover after infidelity. Many need couples therapy to do it. Some end. Both can be healthy outcomes. Read what [your first couples therapy session](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/first-couples-therapy-session-online-nigeria/) looks like if you’re considering it.

## If You’re The One Who Cheated

You’re reading this too, and that matters. If you want to repair things:

- End it fully.
- Be honest, even when it’s uncomfortable.
- Answer questions without getting defensive. Expect them to repeat.
- Accept that trust takes time, not days.
- Look at why it happened, ideally with a therapist, without using it as an excuse.

## When In-Laws Take Sides

In-laws can become judges overnight. Some blame you. Some push you to stay. Some turn up and take over.

- Decide what you’re willing to share, and share only that.
- Say clearly: “We’re handling this ourselves.”
- Don’t use the children as messengers.
- Ask one trusted elder to speak, not the whole family.

More on this in [how to set boundaries with family](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/how-to-set-boundaries-with-family-nigeria/).

## If Divorce Is On The Table

*This is general information, not legal advice.*

- **Get information, not just opinions.** In Nigeria, statutory, customary and Islamic marriages follow different processes. A lawyer can explain what applies to you.
- **Know your finances.** Accounts, property, debts, documents. Keep copies of important papers somewhere safe.
- **Build a safety net quietly.** Especially if you depend on your partner for income.
- **Think about housing and school fees** before you make a move.
- **Keep the children’s routine steady** where you can. Tell them in simple words that it isn’t their fault, and don’t speak badly of the other parent in front of them.

## Divorce Is A Loss, Too

Even if leaving is the right call, you may grieve. The marriage, the plans, the person you thought they were. In Nigeria there is also stigma: “failed marriage,” “divorcee.” Be gentle with yourself. Grief doesn’t follow a timeline. See [how to deal with grief in Nigeria](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/how-to-deal-with-grief-nigeria/).

## When To Get Support

Please consider talking to someone if:

- You can’t sleep or eat for days
- You’re crying most days and it isn’t easing
- You’re drinking or using more to cope
- You can’t stop thinking about it or checking their phone
- You feel hopeless, or have thoughts of not wanting to be here. Please talk to someone today
- You can’t decide what to do

It’s 3am. The generator has finally gone off, or it hasn’t. Your body is wrecked, but your mind is running tomorrow’s meeting, the rent, the message your boss sent at 11pm.

You were exhausted at 6pm. You’re wide awake now.

Then the alarm goes off at 5am, and you start again.

If that’s your week, you’re not lazy and you’re not “just stressed.” You may be dealing with burnout, insomnia, or both. They feed each other, and a weekend of rest usually won’t fix it.

## What Burnout Actually Is

The World Health Organization describes burnout as the result of chronic workplace stress that hasn’t been successfully managed. It lists three signs:

1. **Exhaustion.** Your energy is gone, and sleep doesn’t bring it back.
2. **Distance from your job.** You feel cynical, numb, or like you don’t care anymore.
3. **Feeling less effective.** Things that used to be easy now take double the effort.

WHO places burnout in the work context. If the exhaustion and low mood follow you everywhere, not just to work, it could also be depression or anxiety. Our guide on [signs you need therapy](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/signs-you-need-therapy-nigeria-2026/) can help you tell the difference.

## Why You’re Tired But Can’t Sleep

Stress keeps your body in alert mode. Your heart rate stays up, your mind keeps scanning for the next problem, and when you finally lie down, nothing switches off.

Then it loops. You sleep badly, so you feel worse the next day. You start worrying about sleep itself. Bedtime becomes something you dread, and the worry keeps you awake.

Add the Nigerian extras:

- A 4am wake-up to beat traffic
- Doing two people’s jobs because colleagues have left (more on that in [career anxiety and JAPA pressure](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/career-anxiety-japa-pressure-nigeria/))
- Work WhatsApp messages that arrive at all hours
- Side hustles on top of the main job
- Heat, mosquitoes, power cuts and generator noise

## Signs To Watch For

**Burnout**
- Dreading Monday from Saturday
- Irritable with colleagues, family, even strangers
- Can’t concentrate, making silly mistakes
- Headaches, stomach issues, frequent sickness
- Feeling like nothing you do at work matters

**Insomnia**
- Takes a long time to fall asleep
- Waking at 2am or 3am and can’t get back to sleep
- Sleeping a full night and still waking up tired
- Needing three cups of coffee to function

When trouble sleeping happens at least three nights a week for three months or more, doctors call it chronic insomnia. It’s common, and it’s treatable.

## What Actually Helps

Not a fancy routine. Small, boring habits that you repeat.

**1. Wake up at the same time every day.** Weekends too. A steady wake-up time trains your body clock better than anything else.

**2. Set a hard stop for work.** Pick a time, say 9pm, after which you don’t check work messages. Mute the group chat. If your job truly needs you on call, ask for clear rules about what counts as an emergency.

**3. Do a brain dump before bed.** Write tomorrow’s to-do list and anything you’re worrying about. It sounds too simple. It works because the thoughts have somewhere to go.

**4. Wind down for 30 to 60 minutes.** Dim the lights, put the phone away from the bed, do something dull. Not work. Not scrolling.

**5. Don’t lie there fighting it.** If you’ve been awake for a long while, get up, sit somewhere with low light, do something calm, and go back when you feel sleepy. Staying in bed frustrated teaches your brain that bed is for worrying.

**6. Make the room easier to sleep in.** Cooler, darker, and as quiet as you can get it. Earplugs help with generator noise. Sort out the mosquitoes.

**7. Cut caffeine after midday.**

**8. Don’t knock yourself out.** Alcohol, codeine syrup, tramadol, or someone else’s sleeping tablets can feel like they help. They make sleep worse over time and can lead to dependence. If this is already happening, read [alcohol, tramadol and codeine in Nigeria](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/alcohol-tramadol-addiction-nigeria/).

**9. Build small breaks into the day.** Lunch away from your desk. A 5-minute walk. Drinking water. These aren’t luxuries. Burnout grows when you never get a pause.

## The Bigger Fix: The Workload

Sleep habits help, but if the job itself is draining you, you can’t sleep your way out of it. Ask yourself:

- Is my workload realistic for one person?
- Can I talk to my manager about priorities?
- Is this a bad season, or has it become the norm?
- Is my boss part of the problem? See [toxic boss and workplace bullying](https://www.mindmed.com.ng/blog/toxic-boss-workplace-bullying-nigeria/).

Sometimes the answer is a conversation. Sometimes it’s boundaries. Sometimes it’s a plan to leave. All three are valid.

## When To Get Help

See a doctor or therapist if:

- Poor sleep has lasted more than a month and is getting worse
- You dread bedtime
- You feel hopeless, panicky, or can’t enjoy anything
- You’re using alcohol or pills to get through the day or night
- You snore loudly and wake up gasping. That can be a physical sleep problem and needs a doctor
- You have thoughts of not wanting to be here. Please talk to someone today

For long-term insomnia, therapy that targets sleep, called CBT-I, is usually recommended before sleeping tablets. It teaches you how to retrain your sleep, and it works for many people.
